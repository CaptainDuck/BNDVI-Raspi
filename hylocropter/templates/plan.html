Before the farm
Nothing here needs the camera, the drone or a GPS fix — it is worked out from the lens geometry alone, so you can plan and print at your desk. Rehearse over a field or car park you can walk: fly the same altitude and trigger distance you intend to use at the farm, then check the photo count you actually got against the number below. If they disagree, the trigger is not firing the way you think it is, and that is much better to discover at school.

What this does not tell you
These are geometric numbers: how much ground a photo covers, how many of them fit, how long the path is at a given speed. They assume the camera points straight down, the drone holds altitude, and every trigger produces a saved photo. Whether that last one is true is exactly what a practice flight is for — and it is the one thing that has never been tested against real hardware here (RESEARCH-GAPS.md §7). Battery figures assume {{ usable_minutes }} usable minutes on a 3S pack, which is optimistic in wind.
